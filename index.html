Sumukha R Bharadwaj

Circumgalactic & Intergalactic Medium • Quasar Absorption Systems
“Tracing the invisible baryonic filaments, cold-mode inflows, and warm-hot feedback halos that govern galaxy evolution.”

I am a PhD researcher in the Department of Earth and Space Sciences at the Indian Institute of Space Science and Technology (IIST), advised by Dr. Anand Narayanan. My work probes the physical, chemical, and kinematic state of diffuse gas in the circumgalactic medium (CGM) and intergalactic medium (IGM).

By combining ultraviolet quasar absorption spectroscopy from HST/COS with 3D integral field unit (IFU) galaxy kinematics from VLT/MUSE—interpreted alongside magnetohydrodynamical simulations such as IllustrisTNG and EAGLE—I investigate how pristine cosmic web accretion and energetic feedback shape galaxies across cosmic time.

01 — Simulations & Sightlines IllustrisTNG • EAGLE • HST/COS

Cosmological Hydrodynamical Simulations & CGM Spectroscopy

Absorption-line observations capture a 1D pencil-beam skewer through a turbulent, 3D multiphase halo. Cosmological magnetohydrodynamical simulations (IllustrisTNG and EAGLE) provide the physical framework to connect observed H I, Mg II, O VI, and Ne VIII kinematics to cold-mode filamentary accretion and SMBH/stellar feedback.

02 — Research Program UV Spectroscopy × 3D IFU

Observational Architecture & Active Projects

Combining high-resolution far-ultraviolet space spectroscopy (HST/COS) with optical 3D integral field unit observations (VLT/MUSE) and photoionization modeling (Cloudy) to decode the baryon cycle around galaxies.

Pillar 01 • Warm-Hot Circumgalactic Gas HST/COS + VLT/MUSE

Ne VIII & O VI Absorbers in Galaxy Pair & Group Halos

Investigating the warm-hot plasma phase (T ∼ 105.5–6.0 K) of the circumgalactic medium traced by the high-ionization Ne VIII λλ770, 780 and O VI λλ1031, 1037 doublets toward background quasars PKS 0405−123 and HE 0226−4110. By mapping the surrounding galaxy environments with VLT/MUSE, we evaluate whether warm-hot absorbers trace virialized intragroup gas, tidal interactions in galaxy pairs, or feedback-driven interfaces.

Pillar 02 • Cold-Mode Accretion & Lyman Limit Systems ApJ 1008, 155 (2026)

Partial Lyman Limit Systems & Galaxy–Absorber Kinematics

Analyzing optically thin and partial Lyman limit systems (pLLS; 16.0 ≲ log N(H I)/cm−2 ≲ 17.2) in conjunction with 3D morpho-kinematic modeling (GalPaK3D) and stellar population synthesis (Prospector, BAGPIPES) of host galaxies. Our study toward LBQS 0107−0235 (z = 0.87641) reveals metal-poor (0.1 Z☉) gas aligned with the major axes of a rotating galaxy pair, consistent with cold-mode accretion streams.

Pillar 03 • Cool Low-Redshift CGM Large Spectroscopic Surveys

Weak Mg II Absorbers & Environmental Demographics

Probing the sub-kiloparsec cool phase (T ∼ 104 K) of low-redshift circumgalactic and intergalactic environments using weak Mg II λλ2796, 2803 absorption systems across large archival spectroscopic surveys. Characterizing how absorber equivalent widths, cloud densities, and metallicities scale with host galaxy star-formation rates and impact parameter.

A Partial Lyman Limit Absorber Tracing Cold-Mode Accretion

arXiv Preprint Title: “A Partial Lyman Limit Absorber in the Halo of a Galaxy Pair: A Possible Signature of Gas Inflow”
Sumukha R. Bharadwaj, Anand Narayanan, Sowgat Muzahid, Jane C. Charlton, and Sebastiano Cantalupo
Quasar: LBQS 0107−0235 zabs: 0.87641 Metallicity: log(Z/Z☉) = −1.05 (0.1 Z☉) Density: log(nH/cm−³) ≈ −3.1 Impact: ρ/Rvir ≈ 0.9 (G1 & G2 Pair) Kinematics: |Δv| = 18 & 99 km s−¹
Read in The Astrophysical Journal (IOP) ↗ View on arXiv (2608.09418) ↗
Abstract (arXiv:2608.09418): We present an analysis of a partial Lyman limit system at z = 0.87641 detected in the HST/COS spectrum of the background quasar LBQS 0107−0235. The absorber exhibits a simple kinematic structure, with the metal-lines and the H I Lyman-series absorption well described by a single component. Photoionization modeling yields a gas metallicity of one-tenth solar and a hydrogen number density of nH ≈ 8.5 × 10−4 cm−3 (log10(nH/cm−3) ≈ −3.1). At the absorber redshift, the VLT/MUSE data show two galaxies (G1 and G2) at normalized impact parameters of ρ/Rvir ≈ 0.9 and velocity separations of |Δv| = 18 and 99 km s−1, respectively, from the absorber. Both galaxies have rotating disks with stellar masses of M* ≈ 6 × 109 and ≈ 2.2 × 1010 M☉. Their 100-Myr-averaged star formation rates are ≈ 2.5 and ≈ 2.2 M☉ yr−1. The absorber is positioned very close to the projected major axis of both galaxies. The absorber’s orientation, kinematics, and sub-solar metallicity (log10(Z/Z☉) = −1.05) are consistent with the absorption tracing a sub-solar metallicity inflowing stream, though a galaxy–galaxy interaction origin cannot be excluded. We discuss these scenarios in the context of cosmological simulations of cold-mode accretion and CGM gas flows around galaxies with halos of mass Mh ≲ 1012 M☉.
